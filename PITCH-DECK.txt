SLIDE 3 — THE PRODUCT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ON SLIDE

    A fantasy sports app where the roster is your real portfolio.

    1. Connect a Solana wallet
    2. Pick a sport — Football (11), Basketball (5), American Football (9)
    3. Fill each position with a tokenized US stock
         Position decides risk tier. The goalkeeper MUST be a blue chip.
         Forwards MUST be momentum names.
    4. Don't own it? Buy it inline through Jupiter — you sign, you hold
    5. Points start moving on the next price tick

    Your wallet IS your squad. What isn't in the XI sits on the bench.
    No maximum squad size, because there's no maximum to what you may own.

SAY

    "The position constraint is the whole game. Without it everyone buys the
    five hottest tickers and there's no contest. The goalkeeper has to be a
    blue chip — so you have to think about defence."

SLIDE 4 — THE MECHANIC THAT MAKES IT A GAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ON SLIDE

    Points are ALPHA, not return.

    A pick scores on how far it beat SPYx — the S&P 500 tracker —
    over the same window. One point per 0.1% of outperformance.

         Stock +2%, market +2%  ->  ZERO points

    A bull market hands nobody points.
    We measure stock picking, not luck with timing.

    Then each sport layers its own events, so the modes feel different:
      Football          every +3% is a goal; a defender scoring is worth
                        more than a forward. Clean sheets for holding flat.
      Basketball        buckets for guards; blocks for a centre that holds
                        while the market falls; team double-doubles.
      Am. Football      touchdowns every +4%, receptions, turnovers,
                        a clean pocket for a QB that never dipped 2%.

    Points bank on EVERY tick. No weekly window. No "check back Sunday."

SAY

    "This is the slide most people get wrong, so I'll be precise. If your
    stock goes up 2% and the market goes up 2%, you score nothing. That one
    decision is the difference between a game and a bull-market lottery."

SLIDE 7 — HOW IT MAKES MONEY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ON SLIDE

    Basis points on every swap.

    Taken through Jupiter's platform fee, collected inside the same
    transaction the user already signs.
    No separate billing. No custody. No extra infrastructure.
    It's a parameter on a call we were making anyway.

    Every part of the product creates swaps:
      - drafting a team
      - transfers — which we deliberately NEVER charge points for
      - adopting a manager's wallet: a basket of swaps at once

    Deliberate design choice:
      SUBSTITUTIONS (bench -> XI) are free for us, so that's where the
      friction goes: 3 free per day, then it costs points.
      TRANSFERS (buying/selling) earn us money, so they are unlimited
      and never cost points.

    Later: subscription for concurrent sport modes and deeper analytics;
    sponsored placement from tokenized-equity issuers who want distribution.

SAY

    "Notice what we charge for. Substitutions cost you points and earn us
    nothing. Transfers earn us money and cost you nothing. The incentives
    point the same way on purpose."

SLIDE 9 — WHY THIS SURVIVES WHERE OTHERS DIDN'T
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ON SLIDE

    Fantasy stock trading has been tried and killed twice.
    We designed around both.

    2015 — SEC shut down Stock Battle for unregistered security-based swaps.
           The fatal ingredient was a PRIZE whose value depended on how
           securities performed.
           Formation has no prize pool, no entry fee, no pot.
           Winning gets you standing, not money.

    June 2024 — India's SEBI banned exchanges from sharing real-time price
           data with fantasy trading platforms.
           Formation has no exchange data dependency. Prices come from
           on-chain markets. There is no feed for anyone to switch off.

    We never take custody, never pool funds, never trade on anyone's behalf —
    which keeps us outside the adviser and broker-dealer perimeter that the
    US copy-trading incumbents needed two years and two registrations to enter.

SAY

    "Every previous attempt died on one of two things: a prize tied to

================================================================================
APPENDIX — QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET, AND THE ANSWERS
================================================================================

"Isn't this gambling?"
    No pot, no entry fee, no prize contingent on securities performance.
    Nobody's funds are ever pooled, escrowed or moved between players. The
    stake is what you already own and keep owning. Winning gets you standing.

"Isn't this a security / are you a broker?"
    We never take custody, never pool funds, and never trade on anyone's
    behalf. Every trade is signed by the user in their own wallet and routes
    through Jupiter. We are not in the flow of funds at any point.

"What stops someone gaming the scoring?"
    Base points are alpha against SPYx, so market-wide moves cancel out. The
    known open risk is very thin liquidity pools, where a small trade can move
    a price a long way — there's a configurable liquidity floor for the
    catalogue to close that, and it's the first thing to tighten at scale.

"Why would anyone come back tomorrow?"
    Points bank continuously, so the board moves while you watch. Three free
    substitutions a day is a daily decision. Following a manager pushes a
    notification when they change their lineup. The retention bet is the
    social layer, and we say so on slide 12.

"How do you make money if people never trade?"
    We don't, and that's the honest answer. Which is why transfers are
    unlimited and free in points, and why adopt-wallet exists — it converts
    one manager's audience into a basket of swaps.

"What's the moat?"
    Not the code — there's no custom contract. It's the leaderboard history
    and the follower graph. A manager with a public twelve-month record and
    five hundred followers cannot take either of those to a competitor.
