# Formation — Pitch

**Target:** CLOCK IN, the Solana Mobile Hackathon. Submission deadline **8 October 2026**.
**Audience:** Solana Mobile judges. Scored on, in their stated order — (1) stickiness and
product-market fit for Seeker users, (2) UX quality, (3) innovation in mobile, (4) presentation
and demo clarity.

---

## Positioning decisions (locked — read before editing any slide)

These four rules exist because breaking them costs points with this specific panel.

1. **Never say "zero risk."** The game induces equity purchases, and those shares can fall. An app
   facilitating securities exposure while claiming zero risk is making an unsubstantiated
   investment claim. Say what is literally true instead: **no entry fee, no prize pool, nothing
   staked, you keep every share.** The phrase to use is *"the game never takes anything from you."*
2. **Hook on engagement, thesis on acquisition.** The dead-portfolio hook is emotionally true and
   lands in one second. But a pitch that sounds like an engagement tool for existing holders caps
   the market at ~190,000 xStocks holders in a judge's head. The Foundation asked for a wedge to
   get a consumer audience to *try trading* tokenized equities — against a 28.4M+ Robinhood base.
   Hook with boredom, thesis on acquisition.
3. **It is a match engine, not AI.** "AI-powered" is table stakes in late 2026 and invites
   suspicion that a model decides outcomes. The claim is *deterministic market-driven simulation* —
   same prices in, same match out, replayable, auditable. That is a harder and better claim.
4. **Name a geography.** Ramzy's ask had two halves — "mobile-first **and geo-specific GTM**."
   A global pitch forfeits half of it. `TODO: insert the launch country and league.`

## Slide 3 — The problem

> You bought the stock. Then nothing happened for four months.

- Holding is boring. Boredom is why retail attention leaks into memecoins and parlays.
- Supply is no longer the bottleneck: **$684M** tokenized equity supply on Solana, an all-time high,
  **700+** stocks and ETFs via xStocks.
- Demand is. **~800K** addresses hold tokenized equities. Robinhood has **28.4M** funded customers;
  Groww has **12.9M** in India alone. A ~50x gap on an asset class that is already on-chain.
- Every product in the category optimises the *transaction*. None of them creates the *reason*.

## Slide 4 — The insight

> A 24-year-old can name every player in the Arsenal squad and has never owned a share.

- Fantasy sports is a validated, obsessive, decades-old behaviour with an existing vocabulary:
  squad, matchday, captain, transfer, clean sheet.
- Formation borrows that grammar and points it at ownership instead of a wager.
- **A slot can only be filled by a stock you genuinely hold.** Playing and investing are the same
  act. Nobody is talked into a trade — they are picking a striker.

---

## Slide 5 — How it works

Three beats, no more.

1. **Draft.** Connect your wallet, pick a sport — football XI, basketball five, American football
   nine. Each slot demands a risk tier: the goalkeeper must be a blue chip, the forwards must be
   momentum names. Hold it already and the slot fills from your real balance. Don't, and you buy it
   inline through Jupiter, signed in your own wallet.
2. **Watch the match.** Your squad plays the market. Alpha becomes goals.
3. **Keep everything.** No pool, no entry fee, no custody. Winning gets you standing.

---

## Slide 6 — The innovation: you score for *beating* the market

The slide the whole pitch rests on. One number, then the consequence.

> **1 point per 0.1% a pick beats SPYx.**

- Scoring on *return* makes the game a coin flip on market direction — in a bull market everyone's
  squad rises and nobody's judgment is visible.
- Scoring on **alpha** cancels beta. A rising market hands nobody an advantage. Skill becomes
  legible.
- Points bank on every price tick, so the leaderboard moves while you watch it.
- **No project in Colosseum's 5,400-project corpus does this.** Every prior fantasy-portfolio
  attempt scored raw return on virtual holdings.

---

## Slide 7 — The match engine: alpha, rendered as sport

> Your XI **3 – 1** The Market

- Your side scores on alpha. **The opposition is the market itself** — SPYx scores when it beats
  you. Beating the market *is* winning the match.
- Every event is derived from price ticks, deterministically. A 3% move on your striker is a goal.
  A 2% drawdown on your keeper is a goal conceded. 1% of alpha is an assist.
- **Same prices in, same match out.** No model decides outcomes. Any match is replayable from
  historical ticks, and verifiable against chain data.
- Mascots come from the ticker. Nike plays on the wing.
- Football Manager proved a 2D match engine is enough to lose years to.

**Why this matters for criterion #1:** continuous alpha accrual gives nobody a reason to open the
app at a *moment*. A match does. It is the appointment.

## Slide 8 — Playing short-handed

> Own one supported stock and you have a match tonight.

- Missing slots aren't blockers — your side plays short-handed, which is a real football situation
  and reads instantly as underdog drama.
- A one-stock user sees a match on day one: **1 v 11**. Scoring is unaffected; alpha averages across
  filled slots only, so a short squad is never penalised.
- And the loop closes itself: the fastest way to stop playing 1 v 11 is to buy another stock.
  **The match motivates the swap.**

## Slide 9 — The social layer

- Every manager's rank, record, lineup and wallet is public on chain, so no profile needs their
  permission. There is nothing to bootstrap.
- Follow someone and you're told when they change their lineup.
- **Adopt their wallet** opens a sheet of everything they hold, ticked. Untick what you don't want,
  set one budget, buy the rest. It copies their *picks*, not their position sizes — the budget
  splits evenly, so you copy what someone thinks rather than how rich they are.

*(Worth knowing: non-custodial mobile social copy-trading via Jupiter won 2nd Place DAOs &
Communities at Renaissance — `destreet`, $20,000. This is a mechanic with a track record.)*

## Slide 10 — Revenue

> Basis points on every swap, taken inside the transaction the player already signs.

- Jupiter `platformFee`. Jupiter keeps 2.5%; **we keep ~97.5%.** No referral program needed.
- No separate billing, no custody, no extra infrastructure.
- **Play creates swap volume as a byproduct.** A squad rebuild is a trade cluster. *Adopt their
  wallet* is one budget split across a whole lineup — 9 to 11 swaps in a single tap.
- Buying and selling stay **unlimited and free, always.** Friction lives on substitutions, which are
  charged in *points*, not money — so capital can never buy a better score.
- **SKR** pays for rendered match highlights, mascot skins and private leagues. Compute and
  cosmetics only, never points.

## Slide 11 — Why Solana, why Seeker

- **xStocks trade around the clock,** so per-tick scoring exists only here. A stock game on any
  other rail dies every night and every weekend.
- **63% of tokenized-equity spot volume on Solana already happens outside U.S. exchange hours.** The
  always-on audience is not hypothetical.
- Solana holds **82%** of global tokenized-equity market share; xStocks volume on Raydium tripled in
  Q2 2026 to **$1.63B**.
- Scoring reads *actual custody*. A brokerage API gives neither public lineups nor permissionless
  copy-trading. "No profile needs their permission" is impossible anywhere else.
- **No custom smart contract ships.** Balances from chain, prices and routing from Jupiter, user
  signs everything. Mobile Wallet Adapter and Seed Vault on Seeker.

## Demo video — shot order

Four minutes, in this order. The order matters more than the production value.

1. **The dead portfolio.** Open a wallet holding three xStocks. Nothing is happening. Ten seconds.
2. **Draft.** Pick football. The goalkeeper slot wants a blue chip you don't own — buy it inline.
   **Film the Seed Vault prompt on Seeker hardware.** Mobile Wallet Adapter is pass/fail; don't
   leave a reviewer guessing.
3. **Watch the match.** Your XI vs The Market. A goal fires when a holding crosses 3%. State out
   loud that it's derived from price ticks, not generated.
4. **Short-handed.** Switch to a one-stock wallet. 1 v 11, still playable, still scoring. Say the
   line: the fastest way out of 1 v 11 is to buy another stock.
5. **Adopt their wallet.** Open a public manager's holdings, untick two, set a budget, one tap —
   nine swaps. This is the revenue moment; let it breathe.
6. **Admin dashboard.** Fees earned. Three seconds, no narration needed.

Do not open with the draft. Draft flows are what every losing fantasy submission demoed.

## Questions to have an answer ready for

- *Isn't this gambling?* No pool, no entry fee, nothing staked. The only thing at risk is the market
  risk you chose by owning the stock, and that exists with or without us.
- *Is this a two-sided marketplace?* No — and this is a strength. A solo player scores alpha against
  SPYx on day one. Jupiter supplies liquidity. There is no cold start.
- *Couldn't a brokerage build this?* Not the social layer. Public lineups and permissionless
  copy-trading need on-chain balances.
- *What stops someone gaming the score?* Only shares held across the whole window count toward
  price return; buying mid-window doesn't count until the next baseline.
- *US users?* xStocks is non-US-persons only. Geofenced, and the target market was always ex-US.
- *What's the hardest part?* Scoring correctness — alpha against the benchmark, per-tick banking,
  session roll-up of role events. It's also the part that's already built and tested.
